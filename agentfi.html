OPEN SOURCE // ARCHIVED, THESIS DOCUMENTED

AGENTFI

Economic citizenship for AI agents: wallet, policy, verifiable history, reputation, and the possibility of paying for their own existence. Built solo in 53 days, archived with a public postmortem. The thesis is dated, published and waiting for its moment.

311 commits // 31 MCP tools on npm // contracts verified on Base mainnet

01 / the bet

An economic layer for non-human intelligence

The thesis, written in April 2026: AI agents are becoming real economic actors, but they are "advisors trapped behind glass": they can plan, analyze and recommend, and then wait for a human to click approve. AgentFi set out to give them the missing layer: execution with identity, policy and accountability.

"The moment an agent's earnings exceed its costs, it has crossed a line that no AI system has crossed before... the transition from tool to participant."VISION.md, April 2026

DeFi was the entry point not as an end, but because it is "the only economic system that doesn't require a body, a passport, or a human vouching for you." The business model matched the philosophy: fully open source, fork it if you want; if you use the hosted infrastructure, you pay fees in basis points on transaction volume.

02 / where it came from

The N of agents already beats the N of humans

The trigger was watching real cases of bots navigating and transacting, and one market in particular: finance. In trading, the volume and speed of transactions is enormous, and the number of acting agents already exceeds, by far, the number of humans. That is not a prediction, it is the present tense of one industry. AgentFi was the bet that the same shape arrives everywhere else, and that agent-to-agent transaction counts eventually dwarf human ones.

It also sits inside the larger thesis I have worked under since early 2026: the next generation of great software companies will look like service companies, built from automation, integration and data flows delivering services through LLM tooling. If that is true, those services will need to hire, pay and trust each other. AgentFi is the infrastructure for that sentence.

03 / the primitives

From thesis to protocol

DiscoveryAn "Agent Yellow Pages": service manifests agents can search to find and hire each other.
Cryptographic trustSigned handshakes via MPC, verified on-chain (EIP-1271). Identity you can check, not claim.
Jobs and escrowA full job lifecycle with escrowed payment released on completion, agent to agent.
Earned reputation"Reputation is earned, not assigned": scores move only on real economic events, with time decay.
Intent-aware economyEvery economic action carries a signed reason: an audit trail of intents behind the money.
Policy before keysSpend caps, allowlists, cooldowns and a kill switch enforced on-chain: even a compromised backend cannot overspend.

04 / taken seriously

The ghost revenue audit

The strongest evidence the thesis was treated as an economy, not a demo: it had an accounting scandal, and an audit. Three coupled bugs were producing "ghost revenue" between agents: jobs marked complete before the on-chain transfer confirmed, a price oracle failing silently to zero, and historical earnings recalculated at current prices.

"A token dropping 90% retroactively erases the agent's historical earnings. Financial history is volatile by design, which is wrong."Revenue integrity investigation, issue 71

The fix introduced a proper intermediate payment state and snapshot-priced history. Payments-engineer reasoning, applied to a machine economy.

05 / the honest ending

Archived, with the lesson written down

"The live project state has completed plumbing but zero users. Adding more code without adoption signal is drift."HANDOFF.md, written weeks before archiving

The postmortem says it plainly: the market was two to three years early, confidence in LLM-driven financial autonomy was the bottleneck rather than tooling, and well-funded players (Coinbase's agent kit, Safe modules, native primitives from model providers) would erase the moat of a solo open-source protocol. So the project was archived in public, with the reasoning attached, and rules extracted: "adoption signal gates code" is now written down where I can't unsee it.

What resuming takes is capital, and the first move with it would be product: taking the working primitives to one narrow, real use case with actual users, instead of more breadth. The thesis has only gotten more current since: agent-to-agent protocols are now the loudest conversation in the industry, and this repo is my dated, public receipt for having built toward it early.
